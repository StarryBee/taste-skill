As the evening primroses began their nightly unfurling and the last rays of the amber sun dipped below the Heatherwick Hills, a sight of breathtaking wonder unfolded across the skies of the Enchanted Realm. The Great Fairy Migration — that most celebrated of annual spectacles — has officially commenced, with observers counting no fewer than twelve thousand luminous wings dancing against the deepening violet dusk.

"It is perhaps the most extraordinary display in living memory," said Elder Thornwick Dew, keeper of the Sacred Migration Records since the moonlit spring of 1973. "The colours this year — a cascade of silver-rose and soft gold — speak of fair weather and abundant bloom ahead."

The migration, which follows ancient ley lines traced by the first fairy folk of this land, typically spans fourteen days, during which the fairies travel from their winter hollows in the Ancient Oak Forest to the Summer Flowering Meadows of Celestia Vale.

"When the wings number in the thousands and the sky shimmers like crushed moonstone, it means the land is well and the magic is strong."
— Elder Thornwick Dew, Migration Keeper

Locals are advised to leave offerings of fresh honeydew and pressed violet petals along the traditional migration pathways, and to extinguish unnecessary lights after twilight to ensure the fairies can navigate safely by starlight alone.

Planting by the Moon: A Complete Guide for the Enchanted Garden

Lunar Planting Calendar — May 2026

The practice of planting by the moon is as old as the stars themselves. Our ancestors understood something that modern science is only beginning to verify: that the gravitational pull of the moon affects the moisture in the soil, just as it moves the tides of the great oceans. During the waxing moon, sap rises upward through plants; during the waning moon, it retreats to the roots.

For the cottagecore gardener, this means simple, beautiful rhythms: plant above-ground crops during the waxing moon, root vegetables and bulbs during the waning moon. Harvest during the full moon when plants are fullest of their vital essences. Leave the dark moon for rest, planning, and soil preparation.

The Mushroom Oracle of Pinewick Glen

Deep in Pinewick Glen, where the pines stand so tall they seem to hold up the sky itself, there grows a ring of ancient mushrooms. Not just any mushrooms, mind you — these are the Speaking Mushrooms, the ones the old folk called the Mycelium Council, each one a pale luminous ivory with caps so wide a dormouse might use one as a parasol on a summer afternoon.

They say that once in a generation, a human child pure of heart may enter the ring at the stroke of midnight under a waning crescent moon, and the mushrooms will speak. They will answer three questions — no more, no less — and the answers, while always true, are never quite what you expect.

Young Clover Ashwick was eleven years old when she learned of the mushrooms from her grandmother, who had learned of them from her grandmother in turn. The family had never quite worked up the courage to try. Moonflower Elderflower Cordial

⏱ 45 min 🍽 Makes 2L ✦ Easy
Ingredients
  • 25 elderflower heads, freshly gathered at dawn
  • 1.5 kg unrefined cane sugar
  • 1.5L spring water
  • 2 unwaxed lemons, sliced
  • 1 tsp citric acid
  • A handful of fresh mint
Method

Bring water and sugar to a gentle simmer, stirring until dissolved. Add the elderflower heads (shake gently to remove tiny insects first — they do not wish to become cordial). Add lemon, mint, and citric acid. Cover and steep overnight in a cool, moonlit space. Strain through muslin into sterilised bottles. Dilute 1:5 with still or sparkling water.

✦ The fairies say this cordial is most potent when stirred sunrise-clockwise and bottled under a waxing moon.

Lavender Honey & Rose Petal Cake

⏱ 1hr 20min 🍽 Serves 8 ✦✦ Intermediate
Ingredients
  • 225g salted butter, at room temperature
  • 200g golden caster sugar
  • 3 tbsp lavender honey (or wildflower)
  • 4 free-range eggs
  • 225g self-raising flour
  • 2 tsp dried culinary lavender, lightly crushed
  • Zest of 1 lemon
  • For frosting: 200g cream cheese, 100g icing sugar, rose water, crystallised rose petals
Method

Beat butter, sugar and honey until very pale and fluffy. Add eggs one at a time. Fold in sifted flour, lavender and lemon zest. Bake in two tins at 180°C for 25 minutes. Cool completely. Beat cream cheese with icing sugar and a few drops of rose water until silky. Sandwich the layers with frosting and decorate the top with crystallised rose petals and a dusting of dried lavender.

✦ Best enjoyed in the garden with a pot of chamomile tea while the afternoon light turns to gold.

Hearty Supper

Wild Mushroom & Thyme Cottage Pie

⏱ 1hr 30min 🍽 Serves 4 ✦✦ Intermediate
Ingredients
  • 500g mixed wild mushrooms (chanterelle, porcini, oyster)
  • 2 shallots, finely diced
  • 4 cloves garlic
  • 200ml dark ale
  • 1 tbsp thyme leaves, fresh
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce (for umami depth)
  • 800g floury potatoes for mash
  • Truffle oil, to finish
Method

Sauté shallots in butter until translucent. Add garlic and mushrooms, cooking until deep golden. Pour in ale and soy sauce; simmer until reduced to a thick, fragrant gravy. Season generously with thyme, salt and pepper. Transfer to a baking dish. Crown with creamy mashed potato, drag a fork across to create ridges, and bake at 200°C until golden and bubbling at the edges.

✦ The mushrooms whisper of the forest floor. Listen while you cook.

Preserves

Rose Hip & Hawthorn Jelly

⏱ 2hr 🍽 Makes 4 jars ✦ Easy
Ingredients
  • 500g ripe rose hips (gathered after first frost)
  • 300g hawthorn berries
  • 600ml water
  • Juice of 1 lemon
  • Preserving sugar (equal weight to juice)
Method

Simmer hips and haws with water for 40 minutes until very soft. Strain through a muslin jelly bag overnight. Measure the juice and add equal weight of sugar. Stir to dissolve over low heat, then bring to a rolling boil. Test for set on a cold plate. Jar into sterilised glass jars while hot.

✦ A jewel-red jelly, rich in wild Vitamin C. A spoonful wards off winter and sadness both.

Candle Making

Beeswax Moon Candles with Pressed Wildflowers

✦ Beginner Friendly
You will need:
  • Raw beeswax pellets (500g)
  • Cotton wicks
  • Pressed wildflowers (violets, ferns, chamomile)
  • Moon-shaped silicone moulds
  • A few drops of lavender or rose essential oil
1.Melt beeswax in a double boiler until clear. Add a few drops of essential oil.
2.Pour a thin layer into moulds. Allow to semi-set.
3.Press wildflowers gently onto the surface. Pour remaining wax over.
4.Insert wick and allow to cool completely — at least 4 hours — before unmoulding.

Embroidery

Botanical Embroidery: Stitching Living Patterns

✦✦ Intermediate
You will need:
  • Natural linen fabric, hooped tightly
  • Stranded cotton in: sage, dusty rose, cream, forest green
  • Embroidery needle (sizes 7-9)
  • Printed botanical template or freehand sketch
1.Transfer your botanical design lightly with an air-erasable pen.
2.Begin with stem stitch for all branches and tendrils.
3.Fill leaves with satin stitch, following the leaf's natural direction.
4.Use French knots for berry clusters and flower centres.

Wreath Making

Dried Flower & Herb Cottage Wreaths

✦ Beginner Friendly
You will need:
  • Willow or grapevine base wreath
  • Bunches of dried lavender, rosemary, thyme
  • Dried roses, strawflowers, nigella pods
  • Paddle wire and floral wire
  • Ribbon in dusty rose or sage green
1.Bundle herbs into small posies and wire the stems.
2.Attach posies to the base, working in one direction, each overlapping the last.
3.Tuck dried flowers throughout to add colour and variety.
4.Finish with a generous bow and a loop of wire for hanging.

Fairy World Terrarium in Glass

✦ Beginner Friendly
You will need:
  • A large glass vessel (jar, cloche, or bowl)
  • Pebbles for drainage
  • Activated charcoal
  • Potting mix with perlite
  • Moss, small ferns, miniature plants
  • Tiny fairy figurines, pebble paths, a miniature door
1.Layer pebbles, then charcoal, then soil in the vessel.
2.Plant moss sheets first to create the floor of the world.
3.Add ferns and tiny plants as trees and bushes.
4.Arrange pebbles into paths, place your fairy folk, and mist gently.
